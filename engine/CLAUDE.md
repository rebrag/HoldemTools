# engine/ - conventions and invariants

C++20 CFR/QRE solver.
Headless CLI: reads a JSON config, writes a local `.hta` artifact, exits.

## Building and testing

On Windows, **always** go through the wrapper - cl, cmake, and ninja are not on the ambient PATH here:

```powershell
./build.ps1            # configure + build (RelWithDebInfo)
./build.ps1 -Test      # ... then ctest
./build.ps1 -Config Debug -Clean
```

Never invoke cl, cmake, or ninja directly on Windows.
On Linux/CI, plain `cmake -B build -S engine && cmake --build build && ctest --test-dir build` is fine (the toolchain is on PATH there).

## The dev box is more permissive than CI - expect green-here-red-there

Two environment gaps have bitten already, both invisible locally:

- **MSVC's headers include far more than libstdc++'s.** A missing `<cstdint>`/`<bit>` builds fine here and fails the GCC job. Include what you use; do not rely on transitive includes. The quickest check before pushing is a transitive include audit (resolve project `#include "..."` recursively, then look for `std::` symbols whose header appears nowhere in the graph) - it caught a second break that would otherwise have failed the *next* CI run.
- **The runner's MSVC and CMake are newer than the local ones.** A newer MSVC emits warnings the local one does not (C5285 on doctest specializing `std::tuple`), and CMake 4.x turns `cmake_minimum_required(VERSION < 3.5)` from a warning into an error. Hence `CMAKE_POLICY_VERSION_MINIMUM` and `FetchContent_Declare(... SYSTEM)` + `/external:W0`: **dependency headers are never held to our warning settings** - `/W4 /WX` is for our code only.

There is no Linux toolchain on this machine, so the GCC job is only ever verified by CI. Push to a branch and let the PR run it.

## Compiler and portability constraints

- **MSVC (cl) is the primary compiler; all code stays portable to clang-cl and GCC.**
- MSVC flags: `/permissive- /W4` with warnings-as-errors; GCC/Clang: `-Wall -Wextra -Werror`.
- No compiler-specific intrinsics or builtins without a portable fallback; prefer C++20 `<bit>` (`std::popcount`, `std::rotr`, `std::bit_cast`) over MSVC/GCC builtins.
- **SIMD ISA level is the `ENGINE_SIMD` CMake option (SSE2 | AVX2 | AVX512), default AVX2 - never hardcoded.** The dev machine (9800X3D) has AVX-512 but the deployment server does not; AVX2 is the shipping floor.
- **Floating point stays strict: `/fp:precise`, and never fast-math anywhere in the solver core.** Reproducibility matters for PioSolver validation; a fast-math "optimization" invalidates the acceptance gate.

## The vectorized-public-tree design (do not regress this)

- Public tree nodes are **public states** (board, betting sequence, pot, stacks). An infoset is a `(public state, private hand)` pair. Private hands never appear in the tree.
- Every traversal computes values for **all hands at once** via range vectors. **Never add a per-(node, hand) recursive walk** - that is the naive tree walker this design exists to avoid.
- Cumulative regret and strategy live in **flat f32 arrays** at `node_offset + action * num_hands + hand` (`InfosetLayout` in `src/solver/cfr.hpp`). CFR is memory-latency-bound with poor locality: keep hot data flat and SoA; layout beats instruction-level cleverness here.
- **The layout is ACTION-MAJOR and that is load-bearing.** Every hot loop walks hands for a fixed action, so hand-major made them stride-`actions` scatters across the hand universe - cache-hostile and unvectorizable. Only the per-hand normalization in regret matching wants the other order, and that is 2-5 elements wide. `average_strategy()` / `current_strategy()` still emit hand-major rows because that is the public contract the artifact writer and the best-response pass read; the transpose lives in those two accessors and nowhere else.
- **Per-hand arrays are sized by the COMPACT hand universe, not by 1326** (`src/ranges/universe.hpp`). The universe is the combos with non-zero weight in at least one starting range after root-board removal, in ascending canonical order, shared by both seats. A combo neither seat can hold has zero reach everywhere and contributes an exact `0.0` to every sum, so dropping it changes no other hand's number - this is **not** the hand abstraction that is out of scope, nothing is merged or estimated. It is shared rather than per-seat because terminal showdown indexes hero and villain into one sorted-by-strength structure, and because the schema-4 exporter takes seat 0's dictionary as the bundle-wide `hand_order`.
- Terminal showdown evaluation with card removal is the hot path. The 2-player path is the sort-by-strength single sweep with inclusion-exclusion blocker correction (`src/eval/terminal.cpp`). `showdown_share` is the side-pot-correct N-seat reference the multiway fast path is **gated against** (`tests/test_multiway_terminal.cpp`, agreement 1.1e-7), not an unused seam.
- **`NlhePreflopGame` (M8a) keeps the board OUT of the tree.** Runouts as chance-node children would make the cards public, which is a different game, and `CfrSolver` cannot express infoset-sharing siblings without breaking bitwise determinism. The whole board average lives inside `terminal_values`: a static pairwise matrix `e2_` at two eligible seats (where the board expectation commutes with the sum over opponent hands, so it is exact in the limit and costs nothing per iteration), and a FIXED board sample at three or more (where the value contains a product over opponents inside the board expectation and does not factorize). Fixed, not redrawn - unlike `SamplingConfig` - which is why the solve genuinely converges and the accuracy stop is not guarded.
  Two invariants there are easy to break and were both broken once: the win side must be a pure equity FRACTION multiplied by `compat_weights` (or it rides a different measure than the commitment term), and **every seat weights a layer's board distribution**, including folded seats and seats committed below a side pot, because they still hold cards. See M8a in `docs/roadmap.md`.
- **`e2_` is projected onto the suit-symmetric subspace, and that is not optional.** Pairwise all-in equity provably satisfies `E2[h][o] == E2[pi(h)][pi(o)]` for every suit permutation and does not depend on the ranges, so the projection removes sampling error and adds no bias. Without it the four combos of one hand class disagreed by 0.11 chips - more than the jam/fold margin of a threshold hand.
- The seat count and seat->agent partition are **never hardcoded** (`src/solver/agents.hpp` is the single source of truth). Only the identity partition is implemented today, but every data structure is N-seat-shaped.
- Utility convention: terminal value = share of final pot minus post-root commitment, so root EVs across seats sum to the root pot (Pio's convention). NashConv is invariant to this reference.
- `CfrSolver::scratch` buffers are pre-sized for the tree depth in the constructor; callers hold references across recursion, so an arena must never grow mid-traversal.
- **The DCFR discount is deferred, and `run()` is the only thing that settles it.** Per-iteration factors live in a history; a node pays everything it owes on its next visit (compounded into one product per sign - exact, because regret signs cannot change while a node is unvisited), and `run()` flushes the remainder so callers read exactly what an eager sweep would have produced. `iterate()` is private for this reason. The history exists because the recalc schedule can leave a node unvisited for several iterations; with recalc off a node owes at most one factor and the result is bit-for-bit the old sweep. Anything new that reads regrets or strategy sums (a pruning pass, QRE) must either run inside the traversal or flush first.
  The trap this replaced is worth remembering: discounting a node the moment its own seat finishes with it is NOT equivalent, because a traversal for seat p reads every decision node (regret matching runs at opponent nodes too) even though it writes only its own.
- **The DCFR exponents are `alpha 1.5, beta 0, gamma 3` and gamma is MEASURED, not inherited** (`src/solver/updates.hpp` carries the table). It was 1.0 until 2026-08-28, which was costing roughly half of every solve: iterations to the same accuracy target fell 2.09x on turn trees, 2.08x/2.46x/2.28x on flop trees at SPR 4/7/10, and 1.59x on rivers. gamma is a **pure iteration-count lever** - the discount is one multiply per cell either way, so per-iteration cost does not move and the wall-clock win equals the iteration win exactly. It is therefore settled by deterministic counters and no A/B noise argument applies; `bench_boards.py --dcfr-gamma` is the sweep. The curve is flat from 3 to 5 and degrades by 8, so do not raise it past 5, and do not lower it back toward 1 to "restore" an old reference number - see M7.1 in `docs/roadmap.md` for the two baselines that legitimately moved with it.
- **Suit isomorphism (`isomorphism`, default true) is a lossless relabeling with ONE compatibility rule.** Member subtrees own no solver storage; every read goes through `Game::iso_rep()` and the `average_strategy()` redirect, which is what keeps the artifact, the C# reader, the frontend and the Pio harness unchanged. Anything new that reads per-node solver state must go through those accessors, never through `InfosetLayout` offsets directly (member nodes carry the `kNoOffset` sentinel and zero hands). Collapse legality is checked per node (board fixed set-wise) and per solve (both ranges invariant, weight-for-weight); an asymmetric range disables the feature entirely rather than collapsing partially.
- **The recalc schedule (`algorithm.recalc`) is deterministic and budget-annealed.** It never engages until the caller feeds it measured exploitability via `set_recalc_budget()`, its aggressiveness is feedback-controlled so stalls self-correct toward plain CFR, and `tests/test_recalc.cpp` asserts the skip count and solution are identical on 1 and 8 threads. Do not replace the annealed budget with a fixed epsilon - fixed thresholds were measured to stall convergence at exactly the threshold - and do not add catch-up weighting to skipped updates - measured to inflate iteration counts, worse than the skipping it compensates for.

## Multithreading: same numbers at any thread count

`threads` in the config is live (0 = one per hardware thread, negative = leave that many cores free). `src/util/parallel.hpp` holds the pool; `CfrSolver` and `compute_best_response` fork sibling subtrees onto it.

- **Bitwise determinism is a hard invariant, not a nice-to-have.** Sibling subtrees write disjoint slices of the flat regret/strategy arrays, and every cross-child accumulation happens serially in child order after the join. A change that makes 16 threads disagree with 1 thread in the last bit has broken the PioSolver acceptance gate, which is calibrated against exact numbers. `tests/test_parallel.cpp` asserts exact equality (Leduc and an NLHE turn tree) - keep it exact; never relax it to a tolerance.
- **Anything the traversal touches must be immutable during a solve.** That is why the per-runout showdown evaluators are built eagerly and in parallel in `NlhePostflopGame`'s constructor instead of lazily on first touch: terminal evaluation is the hot path, and a lazy cache there is either a data race or a lock on every showdown.
- `ThreadPool::parallel_for` is re-entrant - a thread waiting on its own batch runs tasks from other batches instead of blocking - so nested forks cannot deadlock a saturated pool.
- Forking is bounded by a fan-out budget (`threads * 4`, divided among children at each fork) and by `kMaxSplitLevels`. The memory estimator's workspace term is a ceiling derived from that bound; it deliberately over-reserves rather than undershooting.

## The artifact format is a versioned contract

Spec: `docs/artifact-format.md`.
Any layout change bumps the format version and updates - in one commit - the spec, `src/io/artifact_writer.cpp`, `src/io/artifact_reader.cpp`, the C# `backend/Services/EngineArtifacts/EngineArtifactReader.cs`, and regenerates the fixture pair `backend/Tests/Fixtures/engine/tiny_river.{hta,golden.json}` (commands in `configs/fixtures/tiny_river.json`).

All storage goes through `ArtifactStore` (`src/io/artifact_store.hpp`) - open/write/seek/read-range/close over an opaque path.
No filesystem calls in solver code; `LocalStore` is the only implementation, and an `AdlsStore` must be able to drop in without touching solver code.

## Hard dependency rules

- **Zero cloud SDKs, zero Firebase, zero GUI dependencies, ever, in this binary.** Uploading artifacts to ADLS is someone else's job (backend/watcher).
- External deps are exactly: nlohmann/json and doctest, pinned by URL hash in CMakeLists. Everything else (SHA-256, hand evaluator, CLI parsing) is vendored/self-contained. Keep it that way.

**`budget.max_seconds` exists because the artifact is only written at the END of a run.** An externally imposed deadline - the compare watcher kills a solve at `ENGINE_SOLVE_TIMEOUT_SECS`, default 3600 - therefore discards every iteration it was waiting on, which is how a 70M-iteration team solve turned an hour of compute into nothing. With the budget set, the loop stops at the next slice, still runs the EV pass, and writes a shorter but complete artifact stamped `metadata.stopped_reason: "time_budget"` plus `requested_iterations`. Two invariants: **slices end on a BATCH boundary** (`deadline_slice` rounds to whole batches; that is where the discount and lane fold already happen, so a time-capped run is bitwise what an uninterrupted one would have been at that iteration count - gated in `test_sampled_preflop.cpp`), and **unaware phase 1 gets at most half the budget**, so a team artifact can never be a no-team solve wearing team metadata. The watcher sets the budget to its own ceiling minus `ENGINE_SOLVE_WRITE_MARGIN_SECS` (default 300) and keeps the kill as a hang backstop; the deadline is measured from process start, so setup counts against it.

**`budget.stop_file` is the same exit, taken on request instead of on a clock: a solve can be STOPPED without being killed.**
The caller creates the file, the solve sees it at its next slice, and it takes the time-budget path - checkpoint written, EV pass run, artifact exported - stamped `metadata.stopped_reason: "cancelled"`.
The point is what survives: both the checkpoint and the artifact are written at the END of a run, so killing the process discards everything, while stopping this way leaves a chart to look at AND a checkpoint to continue from (measured: a 4-way team solve stopped at 999424 iterations, then resumed and ran only the 51200-iteration shortfall).
A file rather than a signal because the caller is a watcher on Windows driving a child process, where there is no portable way to deliver one - and because existence is a latch, which a busy solve cannot miss and a caller never has to re-deliver.
`stop_requested` (`src/util/stop_signal.hpp`) is fail-safe by construction: an empty path, a missing file, or any filesystem error reads as "keep going", because a cancel that cannot be observed must never masquerade as one that can, and the caller's kill is still the backstop.
Slicing is what makes the check reachable at all, so both sampled loops slice whenever a stop file is set even with no time budget, and **a cancel during unaware phase 1 ends phase 2 before it starts** - stopping the baseline and then training a full team phase against it would ignore what was actually asked for.
It is a property of one RUN, never of the spot, so it is excluded from `config_solve_key` and cannot change which checkpoint a config resumes.
The frontend surfaces it as Stop on `/multiway` and `/compare`: `POST /api/enginecompare/{id}/cancel` records the request, the watcher reads it off its next heartbeat response (`ENGINE_WATCHER_HEARTBEAT_SECS`, default 15, which is also the cancel latency), creates the file, and gives the engine `ENGINE_CANCEL_GRACE_SECS` to finish before falling back to a kill.

**A solve is a LINEAGE with an id, not one run.** `solve.id` (default: a 12-char hash of the spot) names the checkpoints, so "continue solve X" is a thing you can say; it is deliberately excluded from `config_solve_key`, so naming a lineage does not change what spot it is, and reusing an id for a different spot is refused by the key check rather than silently continued. `solve.resume` is `auto` (default) | `never` (start over, overwriting) | `require` (fail rather than silently start a long solve from zero because an id was mistyped). **Phase 1 has its OWN checkpoint** (`<id>.htck.baseline`) and re-runs on every invocation, iterating only the shortfall, so a baseline is extendable rather than frozen at whatever the first run reached - and `agents.baseline_iterations` is a total like `budget.iterations`. The interlock that makes this safe: phase 2's regrets are a best response to ONE baseline, so **moving the baseline invalidates them**. The team checkpoint records which baseline iteration count it trained against, and a run that would move it is refused unless `solve.rebase` is set (which restarts phase 2 against the new baseline). That check runs BEFORE phase 1 iterates - discovering it afterwards would strand the solve, because the baseline would already be past the value the error tells you to restore. Phase 2 always re-freezes from the live baseline solver rather than from the rows inside its own checkpoint, keeping one source of truth.

**Checkpoint / resume is sampled-core only, and exact only from a batch boundary** (`src/io/checkpoint.cpp`, `output.checkpoint_path` or `output.checkpoint_dir`). The solver's entire mutable state is regrets + strategy sums + conditioned-EV accumulators + frozen rows + `t_`, and both the deal stream (`sample_deal(seed, t)`) and the DCFR discount key on the ABSOLUTE iteration index - so restoring that state and continuing reproduces an uninterrupted run bit-for-bit, gated in `tests/test_checkpoint.cpp` and measured end to end (a 200704 + 199296 chunked team solve returned the same EVs to every printed digit as one 400000 run). The one caveat is real: a batch boundary is where the discount and the lane fold happen, so stopping INSIDE a batch splits a fold and the continuation differs in the last bits - time-budget stops always land on a boundary (`deadline_slice` rounds to whole batches), a hand-picked `budget.iterations` may not, and the engine prints a note when it stops mid-batch. Three invariants: **`budget.iterations` is a TOTAL** once a checkpoint exists (a resumed run counts from `solver.iteration()`, so re-running with a bigger budget continues instead of restarting); **a checkpoint refuses a different spot**, keyed by `config_solve_key` - the canonical config minus budget/output/threads/memory-limit/`agents.baseline_iterations`, fail-safe because it subtracts from the whole config rather than listing what to include; and **an unaware team's phase 1 never re-runs on resume** - the frozen rows AND the baseline EVs travel in the checkpoint, because re-solving phase 1 under a different budget would swap the baseline the team has been training against, and the uplift is measured against it. This is NOT the artifact: an `.hta` carries the u8-quantized AVERAGE strategy, which cannot be inverted back into regrets, so artifacts are not resumable and never will be. The vectorized core is unsupported on purpose - its deferred discount history, recalc-schedule state and QRE anneal state would all have to serialize.

## Memory discipline

- `src/solver/memory.cpp` estimates solver memory from the layout before allocating; `solve` fails fast against `memory_limit_gb` and `dry-run` prints the estimate without solving. **Update the estimator whenever per-node solver state grows** - it must not drift from reality.
- **The peak is the ARTIFACT EXPORT, not the solve.** `write_artifact`'s export pass keeps one per-node record (both seats' reach and EVs, the strategy, the per-action EVs) alive for *every decision node at once*, on top of a solver that is still fully resident. On a flop tree that term is larger than regrets + strategy: a 142k-decision-node spot measured 538 MB at the end of the solve loop and 2255 MB by the time the file was written. `MemoryEstimate::export_bytes` counts it and `memory_limit_gb` is checked against the total - leaving it out let a solve that needs 8 GB pass a 12 GB limit having claimed 2.5 GB.
- **Sizing code lives next to the struct it sizes**, because that is the only arrangement that does not drift: `CfrSolver::state_bytes` / `recalc_state_bytes` for solver state, `export_pass_bytes` for the export records (with a `static_assert` on the record's size so a new field breaks the build instead of the estimate). Count heap-block overhead (`kHeapBlockOverhead`) wherever the term is hundreds of thousands of small allocations - payload-only arithmetic lands *below* measured commit at that scale, and an estimate below reality is not a ceiling.
- Every solve logs three memory numbers into the artifact metadata: `peak_rss_bytes` (whole run, sampled after the export pass), `peak_commit_bytes` (private commit, the figure that does not move when the OS trims a working set), and `solve_peak_rss_bytes` (end of the solve loop). **Only the last is comparable to PioSolver's number**, which `watcher/engine_compare.py` reads straight after Pio's own solve; publishing the whole-run peak against it would be comparing different things.
- Sizing rule of thumb: `bytes ~ sum over decision nodes of actor_hands x actions x 8` (regrets + strategy sums, f32 each), where `actor_hands` is the **compact universe size** - so a 15%-range spot really does cost a fraction of a 100%-range one. The same compactness is why the showdown term looks small next to the others: a flop tree builds ~1176 per-runout evaluators, but each is only a few arrays over a few hundred combos, not over 1326.

## Honesty notes (keep these in docs and metadata)

- **The preflop solver is abstraction-free in the HAND dimension, sampled in the CHANCE dimension, and exact on hero blockers.** One refinement to state precisely: the sampled core solves preflop trees per 169-class suit orbit by default (`algorithm.sampled.symmetry`). That is NOT an exception to abstraction-free - preflop, no infoset can tell suits apart, so the classes are exactly the orbits of the suit group and the quotient merges only what the game itself makes identical. It is a lossless relabeling that pools every member combo's sample into one row - but MEASURE before repeating the naive ~8x variance claim: members of a class share each deal's board and opponents, so their noise is heavily correlated and the realized nashconv gain is 4-13% at current budgets (HU 1.13x, 3-way 1.09x, 4-way 1.04x). The quotient still earns its default: exact suit symmetry in the output (suit noise is structurally impossible), 8x less solver memory, and it is not slower. Anything merging hands the game CAN tell apart is still bucketing and still out of the vectorized core.
  That sentence is the whole honest accounting and it belongs wherever the mode is described. In the vectorized estimator every combo keeps its own regret row, strategy row and EV; under the sampled core's quotient the rows are per orbit and every combo still exports its own (identical within a class) row - the 169 rollup stays export-only aggregation either way. What IS approximated: the board in the FACTORIZED estimator (a fixed seeded sample, reproducible from `board_sample`), and stochastic convergence in the sampled core, which deals real boards instead.
- **Chip conservation is the gate, not a nicety.** Root EVs sum to the dead money if and only if EVERY seat integrates the identical set of deals - so any rule phrased in terms of "hero" breaks it by construction, and a half-correction (exact mass, approximate fraction) is the worst of both because the two halves become two different measures multiplied together. `tests/test_multiway_terminal.cpp` gates it per terminal; it is exact (1.3e-8) at two and three seats. **Four or more seats cannot be exact with a first-order collision expansion** - a deal where two opponent pairs collide is weighted -1 by a clean seat and 0 by the seats in the collision - and the higher-order terms are O(H) per hero hand per board, measured intractable. See M8a in `docs/roadmap.md` before trying again.
- **The sampled core (M8c, `SampledCfrSolver`) is how 4+ seats conserve: deal the cards instead of correcting for not dealing them.** Each iteration deals one concrete hand per seat plus a real board, without replacement, so collisions cannot occur and every deal's payoffs sum to the pot - conservation is a property of each sample, not a correction. The hero stays vectorized over its whole universe (hands colliding with the deal zeroed, hero blockers exact); the other seats are pinned to their dealt hands. Deterministic by the same standard as the vectorized core: iteration t's deal is a counter-based function of (seed, t), batches run against regrets frozen at batch start, fixed lanes fold back serially in lane order, and `tests/test_sampled_kuhn.cpp` asserts BITWISE equality across thread counts. Two lessons paid for there: terminal values for deal-blocked hands must be zeroed explicitly (the vectorized core's reach vector does it implicitly; a pinned scalar reach cannot), and best response against a coarse fixed-board evaluator reports the GAME MISMATCH as a nashconv floor - the sampled core solves the true game, so measure it with a fine evaluator rather than more iterations.
- **Bunching WAS half corrected, and that history is worth knowing.** Correcting only the profile mass and leaving the equity fraction hero-only got 63% of the gap to MonkerSolver and still would not conserve, for the reason above. Both halves now use the same rule: `layer_masses` does ONE joint sweep over a board's tie groups carrying per-card accumulators for every other seat and every pair, so the collision term can be evaluated at each hero hand's own strength threshold. `worse_and_tie` and `board_compat` were superseded by it and deleted.
  Two things in there are not the correction and matter as much: showdowns are normalized by ONE shared scale rather than a per-hand `num/den` ratio (a per-hand ratio gives every hand its own measure, which is a conservation error rather than the variance it replaces), and a FOLDED hero is measured on the sampled mass too, not on the board-free one. It costs the 4-way 10bb spot 8.5 s -> 216 s.
- **Bunching is not bucketing.** They get confused because MonkerSolver's abstraction dialog is the nearest reference point. Bucketing merges strategically similar hands so they must be played identically - a hand abstraction, out of scope for the vectorized core (per-core rule below), and one that makes the answer *less* exact. Bunching is card removal between opponents and makes it *more* exact.
- **CFR has no Nash guarantee with 3+ players.** It converges to the coarse correlated equilibrium set; multiple Nash equilibria may exist with no principled selection. Pluribus did it anyway and it worked empirically - state of the art, not a theorem. Surfaced as `multiway_no_nash_guarantee` in artifact metadata.
- **A QRE solve must never be GATED against Pio, and its differences from Pio must never be reported as error.** That is the whole invariant, and it is narrower than "never compared": Pio may solve the identical tree for Nash beside a QRE run, and reading the two grids side by side is how you see what bounded rationality did. What `watcher/engine_compare.py` refuses is `--cross-check` (Pio rating how exploitable a deliberately non-Nash strategy is - the feature, not a defect); root-EV differences are labelled as expected rather than printed as a bare diff. Entropy-regularized CFR's QRE convergence carries the same "empirical in multiway" caveat as CFR itself.
- **A fixed-lambda QRE has a Nash-exploitability FLOOR of roughly `2*D*log(A)/lambda` chips, by construction.** It converges to the QRE, not to Nash, so plain exploitability plateaus and can never reach a tight `target_exploitable_pct`. That is why a QRE solve stops on the **QRE gap** (`compute_qre_best_response`) and prints both numbers. Do not "fix" the plateau; `tests/test_qre.cpp` pins it deliberately.
- **QRE is a modelling feature, never a speed one. On FLOP trees at SPR 4-10 annealed lambda is 1.3x to 1.9x SLOWER than dcfr, and worse the deeper the stacks** (`docs/roadmap.md` M7 has the table). The iteration saving collapses with depth - 1.41x at SPR 4, 1.13x at 7, 1.07x at 10 - while the per-iteration overhead climbs to 1.85-2.03x, because a deeper stack charges the regularizer at more of the actor's own decision points per iteration while the homotopy's fixed head start becomes a smaller slice of a longer solve. Do not repeat the earlier "1.44x faster" claim, or the "wash" that replaced it; both came from shallower trees. Reducing the overhead is not a lever either - four attempts are recorded above, all slower.
- **Collusion solving is research/analysis tooling** (standard published game theory; how collusion-detection work is done). Using it against live real-money tables is cheating and bannable everywhere.

## Out of scope (do not build speculatively)

TMECor / coordination-without-card-visibility, cloud SDKs inside the engine, one-file-per-node output.
(Hand-SHARING teams are the with-visibility variant and landed as M9; the TMECor exclusion is specifically about coordinating while NOT seeing each other's cards, which is a different and far harder solution concept.)

**Hand abstraction/bucketing moved to a PER-CORE rule on 2026-08-30.**
It stays permanently out of the vectorized core (`CfrSolver`), whose whole value is the exact gradient - that is the rule `docs/perf-plan.md` argues, and it is unchanged.
It is IN scope for the sampled core (`SampledCfrSolver`), where it is the planned route to multiway POSTFLOP and PLO (270,725 combos), via the `InfosetIndexer` seam.
Monker's mechanism is the reference: buckets/node = strength buckets x texture classes, suit-isomorphic boards underneath.

**GPU came off this list on 2026-08-29 and now splits in two.** `engine.exe` stays a headless CPU-only CLI - a GPU dependency inside the solver binary is still the line being crossed. But the product direction is now GTO Wizard / Ruse-style **depth-limited solving** (chosen for multiway speed; the product needs on-demand solves, not only a precomputed library), and one of its two routes is a learned value network. Training that network is a **separate offline tool** and may use a GPU; the engine would only read the resulting file and run inference on the CPU. Hand abstraction stays out and that is consistent rather than in tension - GTO Wizard is abstraction-free too, and depth-limiting is what makes them fast. See `docs/roadmap.md` "The goal product" and `docs/perf-plan.md` "The fork".

**M7 QRE landed** (`qre.mode: "qre"`, per-seat `qre.lambda`, optional `qre.anneal`): entropy-regularized CFR as a REWARD TRANSFORMATION inside `traverse_impl`'s actor branch, not a replacement for regret matching. Each action's counterfactual value is charged `pi(h) * (1/lambda) * (log sigma + log A)`, where `pi(h)` is `Game::compat_weights` - the dilation, and load-bearing: softmax is not scale-invariant, so without it one lambda would mean a different rationality at every infoset. Because only the values fed into the regrets change, `regret_matched_action_major`, `row_from_action_major`, both strategy accessors, the artifact exporter, the C# reader and the frontend are all untouched, and the average strategy is still the answer. Anything that changes this must keep that property.

**M8a multiway preflop landed 2026-08-30** (`NlhePreflopGame`, jam/fold, exact at 2-3 seats) and **M8c, the sampled-deal solver core, landed the same day** (`SampledCfrSolver`, `algorithm.family: "sampled"`): each iteration deals concrete cards, so bunching is exact in expectation and chips conserve by construction at any seat count - the 4+ seat product path, matching the MonkerSolver reference on the 4-way 10bb spot within the band. `docs/roadmap.md` M8c has the tables.
**M9 hand-sharing teams landed 2026-08-31**, on the sampled core (`agents.partition` with one 2-seat group + `agents.awareness: "aware" | "unaware"`): the pair shares hole cards (joint infosets over the ~93,769 suit orbits of ordered hand pairs - the exact quotient) and maximizes SUMMED chips. Unaware mode freezes opponents at a first-phase no-team baseline and the team's training is then a genuine joint best response (one payoff, one optimizer - a real convergence guarantee, unlike general 3+ agent CFR); aware mode trains everyone together. Root EVs for ALL sampled solves now come from a sampled EV pass (200k fresh deals under the average profile), which conserves exactly at any seat count and is the only honest EV for a team - per-seat marginals cannot reproduce correlated play, which is also why `final_nashconv` is stamped null on team artifacts. The estimator trap paid for there: during a team hero's traversal the pinned partner's policy conditions on the hero's VECTORIZED hand, so a partner node's sigma is a per-hand vector, not a scalar - conditioning on the hero's dealt hand instead trains each member against a partner reacting to the wrong cards, and measured as a team losing to its own no-team baseline. Team nodes get **two-sided updates**: a partner node's descent folds the mate's sigma into the returned values AFTER descending (same expectation by linearity), which keeps the child values counterfactual for the mate and lets every deal train the mate's FULL conditioned row - without it a conditioned cell only trained when both hands were literally dealt (~0.001% of deals) and the conditioned charts were noise. Strategy sums at team nodes carry BOTH seats' reach (`mate_reach` threading) so exported marginals are reach-weighted and `team_rollup.partner_reach` honestly flags conditionings that never happen; frozen seats export their baseline rows from `average_strategy` (they accumulate no strategy sums of their own - the uniform fallback once shipped 50/50 charts for them). Team solves also accumulate conditioned TEAM EVs (`ev_sum_`/`ev_w_`, allocated only when a team exists - the memory estimator counts 4 arrays per tier instead of 2): reach-weighted counterfactual values over an opp_w denominator, shipped per (partner class, own class, action) in `team_rollup.ev` for the conditioned tooltips. Unaware phase lengths are independent: `agents.baseline_iterations` (phase 1, defaults to `budget.iterations`) vs `budget.iterations` (phase 2, the cheaper one - only team heroes traverse). Team EV is seed-stable but the joint argmax is NOT unique (which seat carries the aggression can be interchangeable, and a team seat calling its own partner's jam after the outsider folds moves chips within the team) - so conditioned charts keep an EV-free mixing band; gate team convergence on EV agreement, never on strategy equality. `docs/roadmap.md` M9 has the numbers, including the finding that being KNOWN to collude can cost more than the sharing gains.
